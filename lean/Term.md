## Definition: three-branch notation (D.Three)

We define the set $`\mathrm{Three}`$ as the least set generated by the following two constructors.

```math
\begin{aligned}
&\mathsf{Z} \in \mathrm{Three}, \cr
&a \in \mathbb{N},\ b \in \mathrm{Three},\ c \in \mathrm{Three}
   \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathsf{P}(a,b,c) \in \mathrm{Three}.
\end{aligned}
```

The constructors are injective and their images are pairwise disjoint: that is, $`\mathsf{Z} \ne \mathsf{P}(a,b,c)`$,
and $`\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) = \mathsf{P}(a',b',c')`$ implies $`a = a'`$, $`b = b'`$ and $`c = c'`$.

Minimality is used in the form of the following induction principle. If a predicate $`\Phi`$ on
$`\mathrm{Three}`$ satisfies $`\Phi(\mathsf{Z})`$ and satisfies
$`\Phi(b) \wedge \Phi(c) \to \Phi(\mathsf{P}(a,b,c))`$ for all $`a, b, c`$, then
$`\forall t \in \mathrm{Three},\ \Phi(t)`$ holds.
Below we call this induction on the structure of terms.

We read $`\mathsf{P}(a,b,c)`$ as $`p_a(b) + c`$, and call $`a`$ the **subscript**, $`b`$ the **argument**
and $`c`$ the **successor sum**.

<a id="d-olt"></a>
## Definition: subscript-first lexicographic order (D.olt)

We define the relation $`\prec\ \subseteq \mathrm{Three} \times \mathrm{Three}`$ by distinguishing cases on the constructors of both arguments.

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Z} &\prec \mathsf{Z} &&:\iff \bot, \cr
\mathsf{Z} &\prec \mathsf{P}(a,b,c) &&:\iff \top, \cr
\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) &\prec \mathsf{Z} &&:\iff \bot, \cr
\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) &\prec \mathsf{P}(e,f,g) &&:\iff
  a \lt e \ \vee\ (a = e \wedge b \prec f) \ \vee\ (a = e \wedge b = f \wedge c \prec g).
\end{aligned}
```

The recursive calls on the right-hand side of the fourth clause are $`b \prec f`$ and $`c \prec g`$, and in
both of them the arguments are proper subterms of $`\mathsf{P}(a,b,c)`$ and $`\mathsf{P}(e,f,g)`$; hence this definition is well defined.

Thus $`\prec`$ is the lexicographic order that compares principal terms in the order subscript, argument, successor sum.

<a id="d-ole"></a>
## Definition: non-strict order (D.ole)

```math
x \preceq y :\iff x \prec y \ \vee\ x = y .
```

<a id="t-olt_Z_Z"></a>
## Theorem: $`\mathsf{Z}`$ is incomparable with $`\mathsf{Z}`$ (T.olt_Z_Z)

### Theorem

$`\neg(\mathsf{Z} \prec \mathsf{Z})`$.

### Proof

By the first clause of the definition of $`\prec`$ (D.olt), $`\mathsf{Z} \prec \mathsf{Z}`$ is the same proposition as $`\bot`$ by definition.
Hence assuming it yields $`\bot`$. ∎

<a id="t-olt_Z_P"></a>
## Theorem: $`\mathsf{Z}`$ is smaller than every principal term (T.olt_Z_P)

### Theorem

For all $`a \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`b, c \in \mathrm{Three}`$, $`\mathsf{Z} \prec \mathsf{P}(a,b,c)`$.

### Proof

By the second clause of the definition of $`\prec`$ (D.olt), $`\mathsf{Z} \prec \mathsf{P}(a,b,c)`$ is the same
proposition as $`\top`$ by definition, and $`\top`$ holds. ∎

<a id="t-olt_P_Z"></a>
## Theorem: no principal term is smaller than $`\mathsf{Z}`$ (T.olt_P_Z)

### Theorem

For all $`a \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`b, c \in \mathrm{Three}`$, $`\neg\bigl(\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) \prec \mathsf{Z}\bigr)`$.

### Proof

By the third clause of the definition of $`\prec`$ (D.olt), $`\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) \prec \mathsf{Z}`$ is the same
proposition as $`\bot`$ by definition. ∎

<a id="t-olt_P_P"></a>
## Theorem: comparison of two principal terms (T.olt_P_P)

### Theorem

For all $`a, e \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`b, c, f, g \in \mathrm{Three}`$,

```math
\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) \prec \mathsf{P}(e,f,g) \iff
  a \lt e \ \vee\ (a = e \wedge b \prec f) \ \vee\ (a = e \wedge b = f \wedge c \prec g).
```

### Proof

This is exactly the fourth clause of the definition of $`\prec`$ (D.olt), and the two sides are the same proposition by definition. ∎

## Definition: leading subscript (D.lead)

```math
\mathrm{lead}\,\mathsf{Z} := 0, \qquad \mathrm{lead}\,\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) := a .
```

<a id="t-lead_Z"></a>
## Theorem: the leading subscript of $`\mathsf{Z}`$ (T.lead_Z)

### Theorem

$`\mathrm{lead}\,\mathsf{Z} = 0`$.

### Proof

This is exactly the first clause of the definition of $`\mathrm{lead}`$ (D.lead). ∎

<a id="t-lead_P"></a>
## Theorem: the leading subscript of a principal term (T.lead_P)

### Theorem

For all $`a \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`b, c \in \mathrm{Three}`$, $`\mathrm{lead}\,\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) = a`$.

### Proof

This is exactly the second clause of the definition of $`\mathrm{lead}`$ (D.lead). ∎

<a id="t-olt_P_of_lead_lt"></a>
## Theorem: domination by the leading subscript (T.olt_P_of_lead_lt)

### Theorem

Let $`t \in \mathrm{Three}`$, $`w \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`b, c \in \mathrm{Three}`$.
If $`t = \mathsf{Z}`$ or $`\mathrm{lead}\,t \lt w`$, then $`t \prec \mathsf{P}(w,b,c)`$.

### Proof

We distinguish cases on the constructor of $`t`$.

- Case $`t = \mathsf{Z}`$. By [T.olt_Z_P](#t-olt_Z_P), $`\mathsf{Z} \prec \mathsf{P}(w,b,c)`$.

- Case $`t = \mathsf{P}(a,b',c')`$. The first disjunct $`t = \mathsf{Z}`$ of the hypothesis is false, since the
  images of the constructors are pairwise disjoint. Hence the second disjunct holds, and by [T.lead_P](#t-lead_P)
  we have $`\mathrm{lead}\,t = a`$, so $`a \lt w`$.
  This is the first disjunct on the right-hand side of [T.olt_P_P](#t-olt_P_P), whence
  $`\mathsf{P}(a,b',c') \prec \mathsf{P}(w,b,c)`$. ∎

<a id="t-olt_irrefl"></a>
## Theorem: irreflexivity (T.olt_irrefl)

### Theorem

For all $`x \in \mathrm{Three}`$, $`\neg(x \prec x)`$.

### Proof

By induction on the structure of $`x`$. The induction predicate is

```math
\Phi(x) :\equiv \neg(x \prec x).
```

- **Base case** $`x = \mathsf{Z}`$: [T.olt_Z_Z](#t-olt_Z_Z) is exactly $`\Phi(\mathsf{Z})`$.

- **Inductive step** $`x = \mathsf{P}(a,b,c)`$: assume $`\Phi(b)`$ and $`\Phi(c)`$, that is,
  $`\neg(b \prec b)`$ and $`\neg(c \prec c)`$.
  Suppose $`\mathsf{P}(a,b,c) \prec \mathsf{P}(a,b,c)`$. By [T.olt_P_P](#t-olt_P_P), one of the
  following three holds.
  - $`a \lt a`$: this contradicts the irreflexivity of $`\lt`$ on $`\mathbb{N}`$.
  - $`a = a \wedge b \prec b`$: this contradicts the induction hypothesis $`\Phi(b)`$.
  - $`a = a \wedge b = b \wedge c \prec c`$: this contradicts the induction hypothesis $`\Phi(c)`$.

  Each case is contradictory, so $`\Phi(\mathsf{P}(a,b,c))`$. ∎

<a id="t-not_olt_Z"></a>
## Theorem: $`\mathsf{Z}`$ is least (T.not_olt_Z)

### Theorem

For all $`x \in \mathrm{Three}`$, $`\neg(x \prec \mathsf{Z})`$.

### Proof

We distinguish cases on the constructor of $`x`$. For $`x = \mathsf{Z}`$ this is [T.olt_Z_Z](#t-olt_Z_Z),
and for $`x = \mathsf{P}(a,b,c)`$ it is [T.olt_P_Z](#t-olt_P_Z). ∎

<a id="t-olt_trans"></a>
## Theorem: transitivity (T.olt_trans)

### Theorem

If $`x \prec y`$ and $`y \prec z`$, then $`x \prec z`$.

### Proof

We argue by induction on the structure of $`z`$ (with $`x`$ and $`y`$ left universally quantified). The induction predicate is

```math
\Phi(z) :\equiv \forall x, y \in \mathrm{Three},\ (x \prec y \wedge y \prec z) \to x \prec z .
```

- **Base case** $`z = \mathsf{Z}`$: the hypothesis $`y \prec \mathsf{Z}`$ contradicts [T.not_olt_Z](#t-not_olt_Z).
  Hence the antecedent is false and $`\Phi(\mathsf{Z})`$ holds.

- **Inductive step** $`z = \mathsf{P}(c_1,c_2,c_3)`$: assume $`\Phi(c_2)`$ and $`\Phi(c_3)`$.
  Take $`x, y`$ with $`x \prec y`$ and $`y \prec \mathsf{P}(c_1,c_2,c_3)`$.

  If $`x = \mathsf{Z}`$, the conclusion holds by [T.olt_Z_P](#t-olt_Z_P).
  So from now on let $`x = \mathsf{P}(a_1,a_2,a_3)`$. If $`y = \mathsf{Z}`$, then $`x \prec \mathsf{Z}`$
  contradicts [T.not_olt_Z](#t-not_olt_Z), so $`y`$ can be written as $`y = \mathsf{P}(e_1,e_2,e_3)`$.

  By [T.olt_P_P](#t-olt_P_P), $`x \prec y`$ is one of the following.

  - (1) $`a_1 \lt e_1`$
  - (2) $`a_1 = e_1 \wedge a_2 \prec e_2`$
  - (3) $`a_1 = e_1 \wedge a_2 = e_2 \wedge a_3 \prec e_3`$

  Likewise, $`y \prec z`$ is one of the following.

  - (I) $`e_1 \lt c_1`$
  - (II) $`e_1 = c_1 \wedge e_2 \prec c_2`$
  - (III) $`e_1 = c_1 \wedge e_2 = c_2 \wedge e_3 \prec c_3`$

  For all nine combinations we exhibit which disjunct on the right-hand side of [T.olt_P_P](#t-olt_P_P) holds.

  | | (I) | (II) | (III) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **(1)** | $`a_1 \lt c_1`$ | $`a_1 \lt c_1`$ | $`a_1 \lt c_1`$ |
  | **(2)** | $`a_1 \lt c_1`$ | $`a_1 = c_1 \wedge a_2 \prec c_2`$ | $`a_1 = c_1 \wedge a_2 \prec c_2`$ |
  | **(3)** | $`a_1 \lt c_1`$ | $`a_1 = c_1 \wedge a_2 \prec c_2`$ | $`a_1 = c_1 \wedge a_2 = c_2 \wedge a_3 \prec c_3`$ |

  The justification of each cell is as follows.

  - **(1)(I)**: from $`a_1 \lt e_1`$ and $`e_1 \lt c_1`$, transitivity of $`\lt`$ on $`\mathbb{N}`$ gives $`a_1 \lt c_1`$.
  - **(1)(II)**, **(1)(III)**: substituting $`e_1 = c_1`$ into $`a_1 \lt e_1`$ gives $`a_1 \lt c_1`$.
  - **(2)(I)**, **(3)(I)**: substituting $`a_1 = e_1`$ into $`e_1 \lt c_1`$ gives $`a_1 \lt c_1`$.
  - **(2)(II)**: here $`a_1 = e_1 = c_1`$, and applying the induction hypothesis $`\Phi(c_2)`$ to
    $`a_2 \prec e_2`$ and $`e_2 \prec c_2`$ gives $`a_2 \prec c_2`$.
  - **(2)(III)**: since $`a_1 = e_1 = c_1`$ and $`e_2 = c_2`$, we have $`a_2 \prec e_2 = c_2`$.
  - **(3)(II)**: since $`a_1 = e_1 = c_1`$ and $`a_2 = e_2`$, we have $`a_2 = e_2 \prec c_2`$.
  - **(3)(III)**: here $`a_1 = e_1 = c_1`$ and $`a_2 = e_2 = c_2`$, and applying the induction hypothesis
    $`\Phi(c_3)`$ to $`a_3 \prec e_3`$ and $`e_3 \prec c_3`$ gives $`a_3 \prec c_3`$.

  In every case $`x \prec z`$ is obtained, so $`\Phi(\mathsf{P}(c_1,c_2,c_3))`$. ∎

<a id="t-olt_ole_trans"></a>
## Theorem: composition of the strict and the non-strict order (T.olt_ole_trans)

### Theorem

If $`x \prec y`$ and $`y \preceq z`$, then $`x \prec z`$.

### Proof

By the definition of $`\preceq`$ (D.ole), $`y \preceq z`$ means $`y \prec z`$ or $`y = z`$.
In the former case, apply [T.olt_trans](#t-olt_trans) to $`x \prec y`$ and $`y \prec z`$.
In the latter case, rewriting $`z`$ as $`y`$ makes the claim exactly the hypothesis $`x \prec y`$. ∎

<a id="t-olt_P_b"></a>
## Theorem: strict monotonicity in the argument (T.olt_P_b)

### Theorem

If $`b_1 \prec b_2`$, then for all $`a \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`c_1, c_2 \in \mathrm{Three}`$,
$`\mathsf{P}(a,b_1,c_1) \prec \mathsf{P}(a,b_2,c_2)`$.

### Proof

The second disjunct $`a = a \wedge b_1 \prec b_2`$ on the right-hand side of [T.olt_P_P](#t-olt_P_P)
holds, by reflexivity of $`=`$ and by the hypothesis. ∎

<a id="t-olt_P_c"></a>
## Theorem: strict monotonicity in the successor sum (T.olt_P_c)

### Theorem

If $`c_1 \prec c_2`$, then for all $`a \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`b \in \mathrm{Three}`$,
$`\mathsf{P}(a,b,c_1) \prec \mathsf{P}(a,b,c_2)`$.

### Proof

The third disjunct $`a = a \wedge b = b \wedge c_1 \prec c_2`$ on the right-hand side of
[T.olt_P_P](#t-olt_P_P) holds, by reflexivity of $`=`$ and by the hypothesis. ∎

<a id="d-translate"></a>
## Definition: translation (D.translate)

In what follows, for a predicate $`p`$ on the elements of a type $`\alpha`$ and a finite sequence
$`L`$ of elements of $`\alpha`$, we write

```math
\mathrm{tw}_p L := \text{(the maximal prefix of } L \text{ along which every element satisfies } p \text{)},
```
```math
\mathrm{dw}_p L := \text{(the sequence that remains after removing } \mathrm{tw}_p L \text{ from } L \text{)}
```

By definition $`\mathrm{tw}_p L \mathbin{+\!\!+} \mathrm{dw}_p L = L`$, and if $`\mathrm{dw}_p L`$ is not
empty, then its first element $`x`$ satisfies $`\neg p(x)`$.

For $`a \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`L \in \mathrm{PairSeq}`$ ([D.PairSeq](Pss.md#d-PairSeq)) we write
$`\mathrm{tw}_a L`$ and $`\mathrm{dw}_a L`$ for the case of the predicate
$`p(x) :\equiv a \lt x_1`$. That is, $`\mathrm{tw}_a L`$ is the maximal prefix of $`L`$ along which the
first entry stays greater than $`a`$, and if $`\mathrm{dw}_a L`$ is not empty, then its first element
$`x`$ satisfies $`\neg(a \lt x_1)`$. A predicate in the subscript means the general version, a natural
number means this one.

We define the map $`\mathrm{tr} : \mathrm{PairSeq} \to \mathrm{Three}`$ by recursion on the length of the sequence.

```math
\mathrm{tr}\,() := \mathsf{Z}, \qquad
\mathrm{tr}\,(p :: L) := \mathsf{P}\bigl(p_2,\ \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{tw}_{p_1} L),\ \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{dw}_{p_1} L)\bigr) .
```

Here $`p = (p_1, p_2)`$, and $`p :: L`$ is the sequence obtained by prepending $`p`$.
The arguments of the recursive calls are $`\mathrm{tw}_{p_1} L`$ and $`\mathrm{dw}_{p_1} L`$, and
both are subsequences of $`L`$, so their lengths are at most $`\lvert L\rvert`$, hence strictly
smaller than $`\lvert p :: L\rvert`$. Therefore this definition is well defined.

It is read as follows. Regard a sequence as a forest by means of row $`0`$ (the first entry). The leading
pair $`(x,y)`$ becomes a principal term $`p_y(\cdot)`$ whose argument is the translation of the maximal
interval of consecutive pairs whose row $`0`$ value is greater than $`x`$ (the descendants of $`(x,y)`$),
and whose successor sum is the translation of the remaining part (the siblings of $`(x,y)`$).
The subscript is the row $`1`$ value $`y`$.

Induction along this recursion, that is, induction which for a predicate $`\Psi`$ derives
$`\forall M,\ \Psi(M)`$ from

```math
\Psi(()) \quad\text{and}\quad
\forall p, L,\ \bigl(\Psi(\mathrm{tw}_{p_1} L) \wedge \Psi(\mathrm{dw}_{p_1} L)\bigr) \to \Psi(p :: L)
```

is called below induction along the recursion of $`\mathrm{tr}`$.

<a id="t-lead_translate"></a>
## Theorem: the leading subscript of a translation (T.lead_translate)

### Theorem

```math
\mathrm{lead}\,(\mathrm{tr}\,M) = \begin{cases}
0 & (M = ()) \cr
p_2 & (M = p :: L)
\end{cases}
```

### Proof

We distinguish cases on the constructor of $`M`$.

- $`M = ()`$: by the definition of $`\mathrm{tr}`$ (D.translate) we have $`\mathrm{tr}\,() = \mathsf{Z}`$, and
  by [T.lead_Z](#t-lead_Z), $`\mathrm{lead}\,\mathsf{Z} = 0`$.

- $`M = p :: L`$: by the definition of $`\mathrm{tr}`$ (D.translate) we have
  $`\mathrm{tr}(p :: L) = \mathsf{P}(p_2, \cdot, \cdot)`$, and
  by [T.lead_P](#t-lead_P) its leading subscript is $`p_2`$. ∎

## Theorem: take on a prepended sequence all of whose elements satisfy the condition (T.takeWhile_append_all)

### Theorem

Let $`p`$ be a predicate on elements and let $`xs, ys`$ be sequences. If every element of $`xs`$ satisfies $`p`$, then

```math
\mathrm{tw}_p(xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys) = xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} \mathrm{tw}_p\,ys .
```


### Proof

Since $`\mathrm{tw}_p`$ takes elements from the front as long as they satisfy $`p`$, on the concatenation
$`xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys`$ it stops at the place where $`p`$ fails if $`p`$ fails somewhere in $`xs`$, and
if $`p`$ never fails it takes all of $`xs`$ and then goes on scanning $`ys`$. That is,

```math
\mathrm{tw}_p(xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys) = \begin{cases}
xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} \mathrm{tw}_p\,ys & (\mathrm{tw}_p\,xs = xs) \cr
\mathrm{tw}_p\,xs & (\text{otherwise})
\end{cases}
```

By hypothesis every element satisfies $`p`$, so $`\mathrm{tw}_p\,xs = xs`$ and we are in the first case. ∎

<a id="t-dropWhile_append_all"></a>
## Theorem: drop on a prepended sequence all of whose elements satisfy the condition (T.dropWhile_append_all)

### Theorem

If every element of $`xs`$ satisfies $`p`$, then
$`\mathrm{dw}_p(xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys) = \mathrm{dw}_p\,ys`$.

### Proof

By the same case distinction as in the proof of [T.takeWhile_append_all](#t-takeWhile_append_all),

```math
\mathrm{dw}_p(xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys) = \begin{cases}
\mathrm{dw}_p\,ys & (\mathrm{dw}_p\,xs = ()) \cr
\mathrm{dw}_p\,xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys & (\text{otherwise})
\end{cases}
```

By hypothesis every element satisfies $`p`$, so $`\mathrm{dw}_p\,xs = ()`$ and we are in the first case. ∎

<a id="t-takeWhile_append_not"></a>
## Theorem: take on a prepended sequence containing an element that fails the condition (T.takeWhile_append_not)

### Theorem

If $`x \in xs`$ and $`\neg p(x)`$, then
$`\mathrm{tw}_p(xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys) = \mathrm{tw}_p\,xs`$.

### Proof

It suffices to show that we are in the second case of the case distinction given in the proof of
[T.takeWhile_append_all](#t-takeWhile_append_all), that is, to show $`\mathrm{tw}_p\,xs \ne xs`$.
Suppose $`\mathrm{tw}_p\,xs = xs`$. Every element of $`\mathrm{tw}_p\,xs`$ satisfies $`p`$, so every
element of $`xs`$ satisfies $`p`$ as well; in particular $`p(x)`$, contradicting the hypothesis $`\neg p(x)`$. ∎

<a id="t-dropWhile_append_not"></a>
## Theorem: drop on a prepended sequence containing an element that fails the condition (T.dropWhile_append_not)

### Theorem

If $`x \in xs`$ and $`\neg p(x)`$, then
$`\mathrm{dw}_p(xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys) = \mathrm{dw}_p\,xs \mathbin{+\!\!+} ys`$.

### Proof

It suffices to show that we are in the second case of the case distinction given in the proof of
[T.dropWhile_append_all](#t-dropWhile_append_all), that is, to show $`\mathrm{dw}_p\,xs \ne ()`$.
Suppose $`\mathrm{dw}_p\,xs = ()`$. Then every element of $`xs`$ satisfies $`p`$,
and in particular $`p(x)`$, contradicting the hypothesis $`\neg p(x)`$. ∎

<a id="t-drop_eq_map_getD"></a>
## Theorem: index representation of a suffix (T.drop_eq_map_getD)

### Theorem

For a sequence $`xs`$, $`a \in \mathbb{N}`$ and a default value $`d`$,

```math
\mathrm{drop}_a\,xs = \bigl(\,xs\langle a\rangle,\ xs\langle a+1\rangle,\ \dots,\ xs\langle \lvert xs\rvert - 1\rangle\,\bigr)
```

holds. Here $`\mathrm{drop}_a\,xs`$ is the sequence obtained by dropping the first $`a`$ elements, and
$`xs\langle i\rangle`$ is the element at index $`i`$ if $`i \lt \lvert xs\rvert`$ and $`d`$ otherwise.
The length of the right-hand side is $`\lvert xs\rvert - a`$ (truncated subtraction).

### Proof

We show that the two sides have the same length and the same entries.

**Length**: the length of the left-hand side is $`\lvert xs\rvert - a`$. The right-hand side lists the
indices $`a, a+1, \dots`$, $`\lvert xs\rvert - a`$ of them, so its length is $`\lvert xs\rvert - a`$.

**Entries**: let $`i \lt \lvert xs\rvert - a`$. The $`i`$-th entry of the left-hand side is the element of
$`xs`$ at index $`a + i`$. The $`i`$-th entry of the right-hand side is $`xs\langle a + i\rangle`$, and
$`i \lt \lvert xs\rvert - a`$ gives $`a + i \lt \lvert xs\rvert`$, so this equals the element of $`xs`$ at index $`a+i`$. ∎

## Theorem: in row 0 a child is greater than its parent (T.nextrel0_entry0_less)

### Theorem

If $`j_0 \to^M_0 j_1`$ ([D.nextrel0](Pss.md#d-nextrel0)), then
$`M_{0,j_0} \lt M_{0,j_1}`$ ([D.entry](Pss.md#d-entry)).

### Proof

This is exactly the fourth condition of the definition of $`\to^M_0`$ (D.nextrel0). ∎

<a id="t-le0_entry0_mono"></a>
## Theorem: in row 0 a descendant is at least its ancestor (T.le0_entry0_mono)

### Theorem

If $`j_0 \le^M_0 j_1`$ ([D.le0](Pss.md#d-le0)), then $`M_{0,j_0} \le M_{0,j_1}`$.

### Proof

By the third condition of the definition of $`\le^M_0`$ (D.le0) we have $`j_0 \mathbin{(\to^M_0)^{*}} j_1`$.
We argue by induction on the construction of this reflexive transitive closure. The induction predicate is

```math
\Phi(j) :\equiv M_{0,j_0} \le M_{0,j} .
```

- **Base case** ($`j = j_0`$, chain of length $`0`$): $`M_{0,j_0} \le M_{0,j_0}`$ holds by reflexivity of $`\le`$.

- **Inductive step** (from $`j_0 \mathbin{(\to^M_0)^{*}} y`$ and $`y \to^M_0 z`$ to $`j_0 \mathbin{(\to^M_0)^{*}} z`$):
  assume $`\Phi(y)`$, that is, $`M_{0,j_0} \le M_{0,y}`$.
  By [T.nextrel0_entry0_less](#t-nextrel0_entry0_less) we have $`M_{0,y} \lt M_{0,z}`$, so
  transitivity of $`\le`$ gives $`M_{0,j_0} \le M_{0,z}`$. Hence $`\Phi(z)`$. ∎

<a id="t-nextrel0_index_less"></a>
## Theorem: in row 0 a child lies to the right of its parent (T.nextrel0_index_less)

### Theorem

If $`a \to^M_0 b`$, then $`a \lt b`$.

### Proof

This is exactly the third condition of the definition of $`\to^M_0`$ (D.nextrel0). ∎

<a id="t-nextrel0_rtrancl_index_le"></a>
## Theorem: in row 0 a descendant lies at or to the right of its ancestor (T.nextrel0_rtrancl_index_le)

### Theorem

If $`a \mathbin{(\to^M_0)^{*}} b`$, then $`a \le b`$.

### Proof

By induction on the construction of the reflexive transitive closure. The induction predicate is $`\Phi(j) :\equiv a \le j`$.

- **Base case** ($`j = a`$): by reflexivity of $`\le`$.
- **Inductive step** (from $`a \mathbin{(\to^M_0)^{*}} y`$ and $`y \to^M_0 z`$): the induction hypothesis $`\Phi(y)`$ is
  $`a \le y`$. By [T.nextrel0_index_less](#t-nextrel0_index_less) we have $`y \lt z`$, so
  $`a \le z`$. ∎

<a id="t-le0_interval_gt"></a>
## Theorem: every column from a row 0 ancestor to its descendant is greater than the ancestor (T.le0_interval_gt)

### Theorem

Assume $`j_0 \mathbin{(\to^M_0)^{*}} j_1`$. Then

```math
\forall k,\ \bigl(j_0 \lt k \wedge k \le j_1\bigr) \to M_{0,j_0} \lt M_{0,k} .
```

That is, at **every** index of the interval $`(j_0, j_1]`$ (not only at the points of the chain, but also
in the valleys between them) the row $`0`$ value is strictly greater than $`M_{0,j_0}`$.

### Proof

By induction on the construction of the reflexive transitive closure. The induction predicate is

```math
\Phi(j) :\equiv \forall k,\ \bigl(j_0 \lt k \wedge k \le j\bigr) \to M_{0,j_0} \lt M_{0,k} .
```

- **Base case** ($`j = j_0`$): the antecedent is $`j_0 \lt k \wedge k \le j_0`$, and no $`k`$
  satisfies it. Hence $`\Phi(j_0)`$ holds.

- **Inductive step** (from $`j_0 \mathbin{(\to^M_0)^{*}} y`$ and $`y \to^M_0 z`$): assume $`\Phi(y)`$.
  We prepare the following three facts.

  1. $`M_{0,y} \lt M_{0,z}`$. By [T.nextrel0_entry0_less](#t-nextrel0_entry0_less).
  2. $`j_0 \le y`$. By [T.nextrel0_rtrancl_index_le](#t-nextrel0_rtrancl_index_le).
  3. $`M_{0,j_0} \le M_{0,y}`$. By 2, either $`j_0 \lt y`$ or $`j_0 = y`$. In the former case,
     applying the induction hypothesis $`\Phi(y)`$ with $`k := y`$ gives $`M_{0,j_0} \lt M_{0,y}`$.
     In the latter case the two sides are identical.

  Now take $`k`$ with $`j_0 \lt k`$ and $`k \le z`$. We distinguish cases according to the order between $`y`$ and $`k`$.

  - Case $`k \le y`$. Apply the induction hypothesis $`\Phi(y)`$ to $`k`$.

  - Case $`y \lt k`$. We distinguish further cases on $`k`$ and $`z`$.
    - Case $`k = z`$. From 3 and 1, $`M_{0,j_0} \le M_{0,y} \lt M_{0,z} = M_{0,k}`$.
    - Case $`k \lt z`$. Applying the fifth condition of the definition of $`\to^M_0`$ (D.nextrel0),
      contained in $`y \to^M_0 z`$, with $`j := k`$, we obtain $`M_{0,z} \le M_{0,k}`$ from $`y \lt k \wedge k \lt z`$.
      Combining this with 3 and 1 gives $`M_{0,j_0} \le M_{0,y} \lt M_{0,z} \le M_{0,k}`$.

  In every case $`M_{0,j_0} \lt M_{0,k}`$ is obtained, so $`\Phi(z)`$. ∎

<a id="t-translate_single_tree"></a>
## Theorem: the translation of a single tree (T.translate_single_tree)

### Theorem

Let $`p \in \mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}`$ and $`R \in \mathrm{PairSeq}`$.
If every element $`x`$ of $`R`$ satisfies $`p_1 \lt x_1`$, then

```math
\mathrm{tr}(p :: R) = \mathsf{P}\bigl(p_2,\ \mathrm{tr}\,R,\ \mathsf{Z}\bigr).
```

### Proof

By hypothesis every element of $`R`$ satisfies the predicate $`x \mapsto p_1 \lt x_1`$, so
$`\mathrm{tw}_{p_1} R = R`$ and $`\mathrm{dw}_{p_1} R = ()`$.
Substituting this into the second clause of the definition of $`\mathrm{tr}`$ (D.translate) yields

```math
\mathrm{tr}(p :: R) = \mathsf{P}\bigl(p_2,\ \mathrm{tr}\,R,\ \mathrm{tr}\,()\bigr)
```

Finally, the first clause of the definition of $`\mathrm{tr}`$ (D.translate) gives $`\mathrm{tr}\,() = \mathsf{Z}`$. ∎

<a id="t-translate_block_append"></a>
## Theorem: the translation of a block followed by a tail (T.translate_block_append)

### Theorem

Let $`v_0, w_0 \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`R, T \in \mathrm{PairSeq}`$.
If every element $`x`$ of $`R`$ satisfies $`v_0 \lt x_1`$, and moreover
$`T = ()`$ or $`\neg\bigl(v_0 \lt (\mathrm{head}\,T)_1\bigr)`$, then

```math
\mathrm{tr}\bigl(((v_0,w_0) :: R) \mathbin{+\!\!+} T\bigr)
  = \mathsf{P}\bigl(w_0,\ \mathrm{tr}\,R,\ \mathrm{tr}\,T\bigr).
```

### Proof

Since $`((v_0,w_0) :: R) \mathbin{+\!\!+} T = (v_0,w_0) :: (R \mathbin{+\!\!+} T)`$, by the second clause of
the definition of $`\mathrm{tr}`$ (D.translate) what has to be shown is

```math
\mathrm{tw}_{v_0}(R \mathbin{+\!\!+} T) = R
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathrm{dw}_{v_0}(R \mathbin{+\!\!+} T) = T
```

We distinguish cases on $`T`$.

- Case $`T = ()`$. Here $`R \mathbin{+\!\!+} () = R`$, and by hypothesis every element of $`R`$ satisfies
  $`v_0 \lt x_1`$, so $`\mathrm{tw}_{v_0} R = R`$ and $`\mathrm{dw}_{v_0} R = () = T`$.

**Case $`T = t :: ts`$.** By the second disjunct of the hypothesis, $`\neg(v_0 \lt t_1)`$.
Every element of $`R`$ satisfies the condition, so [T.takeWhile_append_all](#t-takeWhile_append_all) applies and gives

```math
\mathrm{tw}_{v_0}(R \mathbin{+\!\!+} T) = R \mathbin{+\!\!+} \mathrm{tw}_{v_0} T .
```

Since the head $`t`$ of $`T`$ fails the condition, $`\mathrm{tw}_{v_0} T = ()`$ and the right-hand side is $`R`$.
Likewise, [T.dropWhile_append_all](#t-dropWhile_append_all) gives
$`\mathrm{dw}_{v_0}(R \mathbin{+\!\!+} T) = \mathrm{dw}_{v_0} T`$, and since $`t`$ fails the condition,
$`\mathrm{dw}_{v_0} T = T`$. ∎

<a id="t-translate_shift"></a>
## Theorem: a uniform shift of row 0 does not change the translation (T.translate_shift)

### Theorem

Let $`d \in \mathbb{N}`$ and $`M \in \mathrm{PairSeq}`$. Writing $`M^{+d}`$ for the sequence obtained by
adding $`d`$ uniformly to the first entry of every pair of $`M`$,

```math
\mathrm{tr}\,(M^{+d}) = \mathrm{tr}\,M .
```

### Proof

By induction along the recursion of $`\mathrm{tr}`$. The induction predicate is

```math
\Psi(M) :\equiv \mathrm{tr}\,(M^{+d}) = \mathrm{tr}\,M .
```

- **Base case** $`M = ()`$: $`()^{+d} = ()`$, and both sides are $`\mathsf{Z}`$.

**Inductive step** $`M = p :: L`$: assume $`\Psi(\mathrm{tw}_{p_1} L)`$ and
$`\Psi(\mathrm{dw}_{p_1} L)`$.

First we observe that the predicate is invariant under the shift. For every pair $`r`$,

```math
p_1 + d \lt r_1 + d \iff p_1 \lt r_1
```

holds (strict monotonicity of addition on $`\mathbb{N}`$). Hence testing an element $`r^{+d}`$ of
$`L^{+d}`$ against the predicate $`x \mapsto (p_1 + d) \lt x_1`$ is equivalent to testing the
element $`r`$ of $`L`$ against the predicate $`x \mapsto p_1 \lt x_1`$. Since $`\mathrm{tw}`$ and
$`\mathrm{dw}`$ only test the predicate on the elements from the front in order,

```math
\mathrm{tw}_{p_1 + d}(L^{+d}) = (\mathrm{tw}_{p_1} L)^{+d},
\qquad
\mathrm{dw}_{p_1 + d}(L^{+d}) = (\mathrm{dw}_{p_1} L)^{+d} .
```

Since $`(p :: L)^{+d} = (p_1 + d, p_2) :: L^{+d}`$, the definition of $`\mathrm{tr}`$ (D.translate) gives

```math
\mathrm{tr}\bigl((p :: L)^{+d}\bigr)
  = \mathsf{P}\bigl(p_2,\ \mathrm{tr}((\mathrm{tw}_{p_1} L)^{+d}),\ \mathrm{tr}((\mathrm{dw}_{p_1} L)^{+d})\bigr).
```

Applying the two induction hypotheses here turns the right-hand side into
$`\mathsf{P}(p_2, \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{tw}_{p_1} L), \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{dw}_{p_1} L))`$, which
by the definition of $`\mathrm{tr}`$ (D.translate) equals $`\mathrm{tr}(p :: L)`$.
Note that the second entry $`p_2`$ is unchanged by the shift. Hence $`\Psi(p :: L)`$. ∎

<a id="t-translate_ctx_cong"></a>
## Theorem: congruence with respect to a context (T.translate_ctx_cong)

### Theorem

Let $`z_1, z_2 \in \mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}`$ and $`T_1, T_2, G \in \mathrm{PairSeq}`$, and assume
the following four hypotheses.

```math
\begin{aligned}
&\text{(base)}\quad \mathrm{tr}(z_1 :: T_1) \prec \mathrm{tr}(z_2 :: T_2), \cr
&\text{(root)}\quad (z_1)_1 = (z_2)_1, \cr
&\text{(r1)}\quad \forall x \in T_1,\ (z_1)_1 \le x_1, \cr
&\text{(r2)}\quad \forall x \in T_2,\ (z_2)_1 \le x_1 .
\end{aligned}
```

Then

```math
\mathrm{tr}(G \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_1 :: T_1) \prec \mathrm{tr}(G \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_2 :: T_2).
```

### Proof

By strong induction on $`\lvert G\rvert`$. The induction predicate is

```math
\Phi(G) :\equiv \mathrm{tr}(G \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_1 :: T_1) \prec \mathrm{tr}(G \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_2 :: T_2)
```

and assume that $`\Phi(G')`$ holds for every $`G'`$ with $`\lvert G'\rvert \lt \lvert G\rvert`$.

- **Case $`G = ()`$**: the two sides are those of hypothesis (base), so the claim is exactly (base).

**Case $`G = g :: G'`$.** We distinguish cases according to whether every element of $`G'`$ satisfies $`g_1 \lt x_1`$.

**(a) Some element $`x`$ of $`G'`$ satisfies $`\neg(g_1 \lt x_1)`$.**
Applying [T.takeWhile_append_not](#t-takeWhile_append_not) and
[T.dropWhile_append_not](#t-dropWhile_append_not) with $`xs := G'`$ and $`ys := z_i :: T_i`$,
we obtain, for both $`i = 1, 2`$,

```math
\mathrm{tw}_{g_1}(G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i) = \mathrm{tw}_{g_1} G',
\qquad
\mathrm{dw}_{g_1}(G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i) = \mathrm{dw}_{g_1} G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i
```

Hence, by the definition of $`\mathrm{tr}`$ (D.translate),

```math
\mathrm{tr}\bigl(g :: (G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i)\bigr)
  = \mathsf{P}\bigl(g_2,\ \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{tw}_{g_1} G'),\
      \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{dw}_{g_1} G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i)\bigr)
```

and the subscript and the argument are common to $`i = 1, 2`$.
Since $`\mathrm{dw}_{g_1} G'`$ is a subsequence of $`G'`$, we have
$`\lvert \mathrm{dw}_{g_1} G'\rvert \le \lvert G'\rvert \lt \lvert g :: G'\rvert`$, and applying the
induction hypothesis with $`G' := \mathrm{dw}_{g_1} G'`$ gives

```math
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{dw}_{g_1} G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_1 :: T_1)
  \prec \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{dw}_{g_1} G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_2 :: T_2)
```

Applying [T.olt_P_c](#t-olt_P_c) to this yields the conclusion.

**(b) Every element of $`G'`$ satisfies $`g_1 \lt x_1`$, and $`g_1 \lt (z_1)_1`$.**
By (root) we also have $`g_1 \lt (z_2)_1`$. Combined with (r1), every element $`x`$ of $`T_1`$
satisfies $`g_1 \lt (z_1)_1 \le x_1`$, so every element of $`z_1 :: T_1`$ satisfies $`g_1 \lt x_1`$.
The same holds for $`z_2 :: T_2`$ by (r2). Therefore every element of
$`G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i`$ satisfies $`g_1 \lt x_1`$, and
[T.translate_single_tree](#t-translate_single_tree) gives

```math
\mathrm{tr}\bigl(g :: (G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i)\bigr)
  = \mathsf{P}\bigl(g_2,\ \mathrm{tr}(G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i),\ \mathsf{Z}\bigr)
```

Since $`\lvert G'\rvert \lt \lvert g :: G'\rvert`$, applying the induction hypothesis to $`G'`$ gives

```math
\mathrm{tr}(G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_1 :: T_1) \prec \mathrm{tr}(G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_2 :: T_2)
```

Applying [T.olt_P_b](#t-olt_P_b) to this yields the conclusion.

**(c) Every element of $`G'`$ satisfies $`g_1 \lt x_1`$, and $`\neg\bigl(g_1 \lt (z_1)_1\bigr)`$.**
By (root) we also have $`\neg\bigl(g_1 \lt (z_2)_1\bigr)`$.
By [T.takeWhile_append_all](#t-takeWhile_append_all) and
[T.dropWhile_append_all](#t-dropWhile_append_all), for both $`i = 1, 2`$,

```math
\mathrm{tw}_{g_1}(G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i) = G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} \mathrm{tw}_{g_1}(z_i :: T_i),
\qquad
\mathrm{dw}_{g_1}(G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i) = \mathrm{dw}_{g_1}(z_i :: T_i)
```

and since the head $`z_i`$ of $`z_i :: T_i`$ fails the predicate,
$`\mathrm{tw}_{g_1}(z_i :: T_i) = ()`$ and $`\mathrm{dw}_{g_1}(z_i :: T_i) = z_i :: T_i`$.
Hence

```math
\mathrm{tr}\bigl(g :: (G' \mathbin{+\!\!+} z_i :: T_i)\bigr)
  = \mathsf{P}\bigl(g_2,\ \mathrm{tr}\,G',\ \mathrm{tr}(z_i :: T_i)\bigr)
```

and the subscript and the argument are common to $`i = 1, 2`$. Applying
[T.olt_P_c](#t-olt_P_c) to hypothesis (base) yields the conclusion. ∎

## Definition: the set of row 1 values (D.sndSet)

For $`M \in \mathrm{PairSeq}`$,

```math
\mathrm{snd}(M) := \{\, y \in \mathbb{N} \mid \exists p \in M,\ p_2 = y \,\} .
```

<a id="t-mem_sndSet"></a>
## Theorem: membership in the set of row 1 values (T.mem_sndSet)

### Theorem

$`y \in \mathrm{snd}(M) \iff \exists p \in M,\ p_2 = y`$.

### Proof

This is exactly the definition of $`\mathrm{snd}`$ (D.sndSet). ∎

<a id="t-sndSet_nil"></a>
## Theorem: the set of row 1 values of the empty sequence (T.sndSet_nil)

### Theorem

$`\mathrm{snd}(()) = \emptyset`$.

### Proof

Suppose $`y \in \mathrm{snd}(())`$. Then by [T.mem_sndSet](#t-mem_sndSet) there is a $`p`$ with
$`p \in ()`$, but the empty sequence has no element. Hence $`\mathrm{snd}(())`$ has no element
and equals $`\emptyset`$. ∎

<a id="t-idx1_le1"></a>
## Theorem: the search row is at most 1 (T.idx1_le1)

### Theorem

For all $`M \in \mathrm{PairSeq}`$ and $`j \in \mathbb{N}`$,
$`\mathrm{idx}_1(M,j) \le 1`$ ([D.idx1](Pss.md#d-idx1)).

### Proof

By the case distinction in the definition of $`\mathrm{idx}_1`$ (D.idx1), the value of $`\mathrm{idx}_1(M,j)`$
is $`1`$ or $`0`$, and both are at most $`1`$. ∎
